## 2. Core Methodological Clarifications

### 2.1 Modeled Business Cost vs. Real-World Retention Reality
* **Illustrative Parameter Assumptions**: The cost evaluation framework employs static unit costs:
  - Unit Cost of False Negative ($C_{FN}$): $\$500$ (representing illustrative annual customer lifetime value loss if churn occurs undetected).
  - Unit Cost of False Positive ($C_{FP}$): $\$50$ (representing illustrative outreach or retention offer cost).
* **Modeled Loss Reduction ($\$40,400$)**: The reported $\$40,400$ reduction (from $\$48,500$ baseline cost to $\$8,100$ model cost on the 667-customer test cohort) is a **theoretical mathematical calculation under fixed illustrative unit costs**, not proven real-world financial savings or verified churn reduction.
* **Unmodeled Operational Dynamics**:
  - **Intervention Effectiveness**: The model does not assume or simulate that a flagged customer who receives an offer will necessarily remain with the provider. Real-world uplift, counterfactual elasticity, and churn prevention rates require controlled A/B intervention trials.
  - **Operational Overhead**: Call center staffing, campaign delivery costs, customer contact fatigue, and margin erosion from unnecessary discounting to non-churners (false positives) are not modeled beyond the fixed $\$50$ parameter.
  - C.R.A.M. is a **predictive risk ranking system**, not a causal intervention model.

### 2.2 Dual Artifact Architecture: Evaluation vs. Serving
To guarantee both rigorous statistical validation and maximal operational utility, Phase 1 deliberately maintains two separate artifacts:

```
models/
├── champion_model_evaluation.joblib   <-- Train-only checkpoint (N=2,666)
└── champion_churn_model.joblib        <-- Full-data serving model (N=3,333)
```

1. **Evaluation Checkpoint (`champion_model_evaluation.joblib`)**:
   - **Training Scope**: Trained strictly on the 80% training split ($N=2,666$).
   - **Purpose**: Enables independent reviewers to load the artifact and immediately reproduce the exact held-out test metrics (Accuracy: 95.35%, Recall: 71.13% @ 0.50, Recall: 85.57% @ 0.08, PR-AUC: 88.52%) against `X_test` ($N=667$) without retraining.
2. **Production Serving Model (`champion_churn_model.joblib`)**:
   - **Training Scope**: Refitted on all 3,333 available records.
   - **Purpose**: Maximizes training sample size, gradient tree binning accuracy, and category representations for future production inference, in accordance with standard machine learning engineering practice.
   - **Statistical Boundary**: The 0.08 decision threshold was selected using out-of-fold predictions on the 2,666 training instances. The full-data serving model has **not yet received an independent out-of-sample evaluation**. Its expected operating characteristics are inferred from the evaluation checkpoint.

### 2.3 Decision Threshold Search Mechanics: Discrete Grid Minimum
* **Optimization Grid**: The threshold search was conducted via `find_optimal_threshold()` across an empirical grid of 100 discrete values linearly spaced between 0.05 and 0.95 ($t \in [0.050, 0.059, \dots, 0.950]$).
* **Search Qualification**: The selected threshold $t \approx 0.08$ ($0.0773$) represents the **empirical minimum among the 100 evaluated candidate thresholds** on the cross-validation loss curve. It is not claimed to be a mathematically proven continuous global minimum over the interval $(0, 1)$.

---

### 2.4 Reconciliation of Historical Baseline Recall (81.44% vs. 82.47%)
An investigation into the discrepancy between the 81.44% and 82.47% recall figures for the historical Random Forest baseline yielded the following findings:

* **Official Benchmark Figure**: **82.47% Recall** ($80 / 97$ churners detected, Confusion Matrix: `[[557, 13], [17, 80]]`).
* **Source of Authority**: This figure is the authentic historical metric documented in `model_metadata.json` and generated by the repository's original `train_model.py` script.
* **Discrepancy Cause**: `train_model.py` concatenated raw files in the order `churn-20.csv` followed by `churn-80.csv`. In contrast, diagnostic re-runs that loaded `churn-80.csv` before `churn-20.csv` altered row index ordering. Because `train_test_split(random_state=42)` partitions by index positions, the file order difference produced a minor index shift in the stratified split (79 true positives = 81.44% vs. 80 true positives = 82.47%).
* **Conclusion**: **82.47%** is the official historical baseline benchmark for the balanced Random Forest on the held-out test split.

## 3. Phase 1 Limitations & Technical Guardrails

Prior to entering Phase 2, the following system limitations are formally acknowledged:
1. **Dataset Generalization**: All models were trained and benchmarked on a single public telecommunications dataset (3,333 records). Performance characteristics cannot be assumed to generalize across different industries, pricing structures, or macroeconomic conditions without domain recalibration.
2. **Missing Feature Imputation**: Median imputation provides robust fallback behavior for partial inputs, but extensive imputation ($>3$ missing fields) degrades precision and recall.
3. **Observational vs. Causal Reasoning**: High churn risk predictions indicate statistical correlation within historical subscriber patterns, not causal proof that altering specific features will retain a given customer.
